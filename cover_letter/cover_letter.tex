Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit ``C2OGate: Externally Checkable Joint Performance Estimation
for Cost-Dominant Continuation Decisions'' for consideration as a Full-Length
Paper in \textit{Mathematical Programming Computation}.

The manuscript's central contribution is computational: a numerical producer
searches for semidefinite exclusions of cost-violating joint stopping-time
cells, while a separate standard-library consumer reconstructs every SDP
coefficient from semantic parameters using exact rational arithmetic.  The
consumer then checks multiplier signs, stationarity, the objective identity,
and positive rational $LDL^\top$ pivots.  Acceptance requires a verified
exclusion for every bad cell; a witnessed bad cell rejects, and every missing
or invalid record is \emph{uncertified}.  Thus neither solver status nor stored
producer matrices are trusted.

The horizon-six flagship is an infinite-class, nonzero-radius case in which 28
rational duals and 448 exact pivots make the joint rule accept while sharp
conditioned marginals reject.  A controlled five-transcript comparison holds
the class and transcript fixed: the joint and marginal decisions differ on
four rows and agree on one retained control.  A second complete 28-cell suite remains strict
after a 40\% enlargement of the proposal radius.  A nonsharing SymPy consumer
reconstructs all flagship coefficients and factorizations without importing
the producer or the standard-library verifier; a deterministic differential
fuzz audit compares 1,956,318 exact scalar coefficients across 32 legal
parameter/cell instances.  A horizon-ten suite supplies the larger
proof-production stress.  Dense horizon-fifteen and horizon-twenty attempts
remain uncertified.  A parameterized H15 implementation reuses one canonical
model across all 136 cells and reduces peak memory by 66.4\%, although it is
3.46 times slower.  Rational discovery is explicitly heuristic
success-or-fail; exact verification is the guaranteed component.

The relationship to PEPit is evaluated rather than asserted.  PEPit 0.5.1 is
the faster floating modeling front end in matched tests.  I implemented an
adapter that maps its Clarabel duals into the same rational recovery and exact
consumer: it certifies 5 of 28 H6 cells, whereas the native named-row producer
certifies 28 of 28.  A separate PEPit builder reconstructs nine complete padded
cells at horizons 3, 6, and 10.  The manuscript therefore quantifies both the
generic front end's speed advantage and the domain-specific representation's
recovery advantage.

The reusable horizon-six certificate is also charged against a measured
periodic heat-inverse workload.  One exact $640^2$ forward--adjoint oracle takes
0.241 seconds in the frozen run.  Across 64 decisions, the gate accepts 32
resolved proposals and marks 32 underresolved proposals uncertified.  After the
entire frozen certificate cost and an explicit 12-call penalty per failed
pointwise saving are charged, its cold risk-adjusted ratio is 0.881 and breaks
even after 56 compatible decisions.  Without that penalty, the measured reuse
threshold is 671 decisions; UCI
and rolling-logistic replays are likewise reported as adverse mean-cost
controls.  The empirical claim is therefore a measured, auditable condition
under which certification is worth paying for, not universal acceleration.

The MIT-licensed artifact, archived immutably at
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21964074}{10.5281/zenodo.21964074},
contains source, licensed data, frozen hash-bound
payloads, a shared-core standard-library verifier family, the nonsharing SymPy
consumer, exact differential tests, and rebuild scripts.  A documented
reusability contract shows the minimal path for a new stopping rule, and
\texttt{make te-smoke} gives the Technical Editor a
flagship-first path before the longer \texttt{make verify} replay.  I believe
this combination of a new optimization-specific certification architecture,
exact replayable evidence, explicit failure semantics, and evaluation-ready
software fits MPC's emphasis on significant computational developments and
technical verification.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and is
self-contained; it does not rely on an unpublished companion paper.  I am the
sole author and approve the submission.  Thank you for your consideration.
