Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript
``C2OGate: Proof-Carrying Joint Performance Estimation for Certified Branch
Decisions'' for consideration in \textit{Mathematical Programming Computation}.

The manuscript presents a proof-carrying architecture for one certified branch
decision. Given a proposed state and an auditable error-and-cost contract,
C2OGate couples two fixed-step gradient continuations on the same unknown
smooth strongly convex function, enumerates their joint stopping-time cells,
and reduces every cost-violating cell to a Gram SDP. Verified exclusions can
accept, an attainable bad cell rejects, and every unresolved status returns
\emph{uncertified}. The paper proves the finite-horizon and exact
dimension-free reductions, states the fixed-dimension rank qualification, and
quantifies the loss from replacing joint stopping times by separate marginals.

The flagship natural-$H=10$ study covers five nonzero-radius envelopes and 330
verified profile-cell exclusions: 198 rational duals are recovered
independently and 132 follow by checked homogeneous transport. For the balanced
source, a standard-library verifier accepts no stored SDP matrices; it rebuilds
every interpolation, transcript, stopping, margin, anchor, and trace
coefficient from parameters in exact rational arithmetic, then checks
stationarity, objectives, slack matrices, and 1,584 positive $LDL^\top$ pivots.
Thus both the numerical solver and producer-side coefficient assembly are
outside the trusted boundary. Rational recovery is explicitly heuristic
success-or-fail, and the generic $H=20$ study remains \emph{uncertified}.

The revision also addresses the relationship to PEPit directly. PEPit 0.5.1
encodes the same cells and is faster in the matched experiment. The reported
$1.54\times$ gap is end-to-end, with Python assembly, CVXPY canonicalization,
Clarabel kernel time, and residual overhead separated. The payload records the
pinned distribution, installer, and public source metadata. C2OGate does not
use PEPit as its acceptance boundary because the independent consumer requires
stable named rational rows without importing PEPit, CVXPY, or a solver; a
future PEPit producer adapter could emit the same proof schema.

To exercise the reusable proof on a nonlinear short-transcript workload, the
manuscript includes 256 rolling proximal-logistic calibration episodes with
40,000 normalized records and a charged 5\% minibatch proposal. Before seeing
any candidate or future full-data gradient, the gate accepts 48 episodes.
Held-out continuation finds zero accepted violations and 48 exact-call
savings. Charging every minibatch gives a prepaid-proof ratio of 0.863; the
cold ledger gives ratio 0.951 and break-even 165 episodes for a 60-second exact
oracle, but ratio 1.392 at 10 seconds. We identify this as a realistic
synthetic workload whose shock distribution crosses the certified envelope,
not as real production evidence or a way to finance a rejected first decision.

The accompanying MIT-licensed artifact contains source, frozen hash-bound
inputs, ten standard-library exact verifiers, tests, adversarial rehash checks,
and rebuild scripts. Exact proof replay is separated from optional solver
regeneration, allowing an MPC Technical Editor to inspect acceptance without
trusting the search solver. This combination of stopping-cell computation,
exact certification, honest cost accounting, and evaluation-ready software is
the reason I believe the manuscript fits \textit{Mathematical Programming
Computation}.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and is
self-contained; it does not rely on an unpublished companion paper. I am the
sole author and approve the submission. Thank you for your consideration.
