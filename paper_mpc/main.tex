\begin{abstract}
Consider a continuation decision between a baseline state and a paid proposal.
Separate worst-case bounds for their remaining stopping times can combine two
incompatible functions and therefore reject a decision that is safe on every
single compatible function.  C2OGate conditions on the observed first-order
transcript, enumerates the \emph{joint} stopping-time cells of the two
fixed-step gradient continuations, and reduces each cost-violating cell to a
Gram semidefinite program.  A numerical producer may fail, but acceptance
requires externally checkable rational dual exclusions for every bad cell; a
standard-library consumer reconstructs all interpolation, stopping, and trace
coefficients from semantic parameters before checking the dual identities.
This gives an exact, dimension-free decision whenever cell exclusion is
complete and returns \emph{uncertified} otherwise.  On the infinite class
$\cF_{3/10,1}$, a complete $H=6$ suite excludes all 28 bad cells with 448 exact
$LDL^\top$ pivots and accepts, whereas sharp separately conditioned marginals
reject.  This controlled decision reversal is the headline result; exact
recovery is currently demonstrated only through moderate horizons and remains
heuristic success-or-fail.
\keywords{performance estimation \and externally checkable certificate \and
semidefinite programming \and continuation decision \and stopping time
\and evaluation cost}

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Optimization methods increasingly combine an expensive, trusted oracle with a
cheaper surrogate, reduced model, learned proposal, or inexact derivative.
Classical analyses ask whether the hybrid sequence converges or how oracle
error changes a worst-case rate.  In applications where one high-fidelity
evaluation dominates runtime, a different question is operationally decisive:
when proposal and certification work is prepaid, reusable, or itself advances
the fallback, can one guarantee that the candidate path costs no more than the
unchanged exact baseline?

The scope is narrower than a complete multifidelity optimizer.  C2OGate does
not construct the proposal, train a surrogate, or certify an arbitrary future
cheap-oracle sequence.  An external mechanism supplies a candidate $y$,
deterministic transcript bounds, and a charged cost; from either $x$ or $y$,
the certified continuation is the same fixed-step gradient method.  The
proved object is therefore one auditable continuation comparison.

Convergence and resource dominance are logically distinct.  An objective
decrease can rotate error toward a slow mode.  A high-fidelity complexity upper
bound does not lower-bound the work remaining on the observed instance.  A
rejected positive-cost proposal is already more expensive if the baseline is
then resumed unchanged.  Inexact-oracle theory \citep{Carter1991,Devolder2014,
Bellavia2019,DeKlerkGlineurTaylor2020}, multifidelity model management
\citep{Alexandrov1998,ConnScheinbergVicente2009,Peherstorfer2018,Kouri2014,
Ziems2011}, and their associated safeguards address complementary questions,
but they do not provide an instance-wise comparison of the two remaining
stopping times.

This distinction is also an identifiability issue.  Oracle-complexity lower
bounds show that every method faces a hard member of a function class
\citep{NemirovskyYudin1983,DroriTaylor2022}; they do not certify that the function compatible
with the transcript currently in hand is hard for the unchanged baseline.
Conversely, learning-augmented algorithms combine predictions with worst-case
robustness \citep{LykourisVassilvitskii2018}.  Their usual object is a
competitive-ratio tradeoff as prediction error varies.  The object here is an
absolute, same-instance comparison after every proposal and certification cost
has been converted to exact-oracle units.  With an unchanged fallback and zero
initial credit, any positive rejected cost makes such a guarantee impossible;
this funding obstruction is part of the model rather than hidden in the
experiments.

Performance estimation (PEP) replaces loose rate manipulations by finite
optimization over interpolation data \citep{DroriTeboulle2014,TaylorPEP2017,
TaylorInterpolation2017}.  General PEP software can encode the same numerical
cell models used below.  The computational object here conditions on an observed
transcript and couples \emph{two continuations on the same unknown function}.
The relevant object is not a separate worst case for each continuation, but
their joint attainable stopping-time set.  The contribution is a computational
architecture with explicit failure semantics, cost accounting, and replayable
certificates:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Verified architecture.}  C2OGate generates
  every cost-violating joint cell, uses only proved presolve rules, and returns
  \emph{accept}, \emph{reject}, or \emph{uncertified}.  The numerical solver and
  producer matrices are outside the acceptance boundary: the consumer rebuilds
  named rational rows from the parameter tuple and verifies each dual identity.
  \item \textbf{New coupled query.}  For fixed-step gradient descent over
  $\cF_{\mu,L}$, the infinite continuation reduces to finitely many exact
  dimension-free SDPs, with a rank qualification for fixed dimension.  The
  SDP decides whether one \emph{same-function stopping pair} is attainable;
  two separately conditioned PEPs return only marginal endpoints and cannot
  answer that query.  Given
  exact exclusion of every bad cell, the resulting rule is transcript-relative
  optimal; the implemented heuristic may instead return \emph{uncertified}.
  Separate marginal bounds can lose an unbounded number of calls in the
  one-dimensional exact-step construction of \cref{thm:class-gap}.
  \item \textbf{Controlled computational comparison.}  Across five frozen
  transcripts, the joint and sharp marginal decisions differ on four and agree
  on the retained $H=10$ control.  The $H=6$ flagship excludes 28 bad cells and
  accepts while sharp marginals reject.  A nonsharing SymPy implementation and
  randomized exact differential tests audit the semantic builder.
  \item \textbf{Measured value and reuse.}  A stable schema separates stopping
  rules, producers, consumers, and ledgers.  UCI and nonlinear replays remain
  adverse mean-cost controls.  A full-grid heat-inverse study measures both
  oracle tiers and shows when one prepaid certificate is worthwhile under an
  explicit pointwise-overrun penalty; it is not cheaper than the unpenalized
  baseline.
\end{enumerate}

The verified envelope is deliberately moderate-horizon: generic exact recovery
is demonstrated through $H=10$, not at $H=15$ or $H=20$.  The paper stops at
smooth strongly convex gradient descent; proximal, adaptive-step, or
active-manifold variants require new exact interpolation models rather than a
mechanical use of the current verifier.

\section{Relation to prior work}

Inexact-oracle and multifidelity methods control convergence or evaluation
complexity for one continuation under an error or model-management rule
\citep{Carter1991,Devolder2014,Bellavia2019,Alexandrov1998,
ConnScheinbergVicente2009,Peherstorfer2018}.  Exact fixed-step iteration bounds
are also available \citep{Abbaszadehpeivasti2022}.  C2OGate asks a different
quantified question: every function compatible with the observed transcript
must make the proposal continuation no more expensive than the unchanged
baseline on that same function.  Minimax lower bounds
\citep{NemirovskyYudin1983,DroriTaylor2022} and competitive guarantees for
learning-augmented algorithms \citep{LykourisVassilvitskii2018,
MitzenmacherVassilvitskii2022} motivate this distinction but do not provide the
required instance-conditioned pair of stopping times.

PEP supplies the interpolation machinery
\citep{DroriTeboulle2014,TaylorPEP2017,TaylorInterpolation2017}.  PESTO and
PEPit automate numerical model construction \citep{TaylorPESTO2017,
GoujaudPEPit2024}.  Exact fixed-step analyses optimize one method's performance
over a class \citep{Abbaszadehpeivasti2022}.
Data-driven PEP--DRO instead conditions a probabilistic performance functional
on a sample of problem instances \citep{ParkRanjanStellato2025}; operator PEP
couples several oracle relations within one algorithmic model
\citep{RyuTaylorBergelingGiselsson2020}.  None of these distinctions is a claim
that interpolation or transcript conditioning is new here.  The new query is
the attainable \emph{pair} $(N_f(x),N_f(y))$ for two continuations sharing one
unknown $f$, followed by a discontinuous cell disjunction.  Running two sharp
conditioned single-track PEPs gives $L_x$ and $U_y$, but it erases whether their
extremizers are the same function.  The experiment in
\cref{sec:joint-marginal-comparison} holds every other datum fixed and shows the
consequence: four of five joint acceptances are lost by this marginalization,
while the fifth control agrees.

PEPit 0.5.1 is 1.54--1.96 times faster on the matched floating models.  A new
adapter also feeds its Clarabel duals to the same rational recovery and exact
consumer: only \PepitVerifiedCells{}/\PepitVerifiedAttempted{} cells certify,
versus 28/28 for the named-row producer.  Thus the reason for retaining a
domain-specific front end is measured recovery robustness, not floating model
speed.  Acceptance uses stable rational rows, and the verifier imports neither
PEPit, CVXPY, nor a conic solver.

Rigorous SDP postprocessing is established: interval validation, exact rational
LMIs, and symbolic--numeric recovery have complementary assumptions
\citep{JanssonVSDP2006,HenrionNaldiSafeyElDin2016,PeyrlParrilo2008,
DavisPapp2022}.  C2OGate contributes no generic recovery theorem.  Its producer
is heuristic success-or-fail; its guaranteed component reconstructs each PEP
dual identity over $\mathbb Q$ and fails closed.  A second SymPy implementation
below shares neither the coefficient builder nor the $LDL^\top$ code with the
acceptance consumer.

The producer--consumer separation is analogous to proof-carrying code
\citep{Necula1997}, but the artifact carries rational optimization
certificates, not machine-checked proofs.  Related work extracts sparse
human-readable proofs from PEP duals \citep{ThomsenEtAl2026}; no proof-assistant
compilation is claimed here.  The term ``externally checkable'' is deliberate:
the consumers are separate from the numerical producer, but their shared
semantic specification remains trusted code.  Linear-operator interpolation
\citep{Bousselmi2024} is a possible extension, not part of the verified backend.

\section{Cost model and transcript contract}
\label{sec:model}

Normalize one call to the exact oracle to unit cost.  Fix a deterministic
baseline algorithm $\Alg$, a termination rule, a current state $x$, and
a candidate state $y$.  All data already observed before the continuation decision
form a transcript $\cT$.  The transcript may include exact interpolation data
from earlier iterations, cheap-oracle output, deterministic error contracts,
and bounds on the location of a solution.  It must not contain an uncharged
new exact evaluation.

The mechanism producing $y$ is arbitrary and lies outside the certified
continuation model.  Its only interface is the auditable contract recorded in
$\cT$ together with its contribution to $c$.  After the branch, both paths run
the same algorithm $\Alg$ and use the same exact-oracle call convention.
Thus the results below certify a proposal contract, not the correctness or
cost of an unmodeled sequence of future surrogate calls.

Let $\mathfrak F(\cT)$ be the set of all problem instances consistent with
$\cT$.  For $f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)$, write
\[
 N_f(x),\qquad N_f(y)\in\N_0
\]
for the numbers of \emph{future} exact calls made by $\Alg$ from $x$ and
$y$, using the same call-counting convention.  Shared past calls are sunk and
are excluded from both numbers.  At this abstract level, assume that these
stopping times are finite for every $f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)$; the gradient
specialization proves this property from checkable distance and curvature
bounds.  Let $m\in\N_0$ be a required reduction in
exact calls, and let $c\geq0$ be every charged cost of taking the hybrid branch,
measured in exact-call units.  This includes proposal construction,
certificate construction and verification whenever they are performed online.
The desired robust inequalities are
\begin{equation}
 N_f(y)\leq N_f(x)-m,
 \qquad c+N_f(y)\leq N_f(x)
 \quad\text{for all }f\in\mathfrak F(\cT).
 \label{eq:two-requirements}
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Joint attainable set]
The transcript-conditioned stopping-time set and its directed support value are
\begin{align}
 \cC(\cT)
   &:=\{(N_f(x),N_f(y)):f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)\}, \label{eq:joint-set}\\
 \Delta^*(\cT,y)
   &:=\sup_{(r,s)\in\cC(\cT)}(s-r).                 \label{eq:delta}
\end{align}
Assume throughout that $\mathfrak F(\cT)$ is nonempty.
\end{definition}

The supremum is taken over a \emph{single} consistent function in each pair;
this coupling is the source of the improvement over separate bounds.

\subsection{Software and reusability contract}
\label{sec:software-contract}

C2OGate treats certification as a pipeline whose components have different
trust requirements.  A numerical optimizer may propose a candidate or locate
a counterexample, but only a verified exclusion may support acceptance.  The
implementation therefore exposes the following interfaces.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\scriptsize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\caption{C2OGate components and their trust boundaries.}
\label{tab:software-contract}
\begin{tabular}{P{0.20\linewidth}P{0.36\linewidth}P{0.34\linewidth}}
\toprule
Component & Input/output & Acceptance requirement\\
\midrule
Transcript builder and interface validator & Cached exact data, cheap
contracts, class and distance bounds $\mapsto\cT$ & Every retained relation

lifting; no uncharged exact call is hidden in $\cT$.\\
Cost ledger & Wall-clock or resource costs $\mapsto c$ in exact-call units &
Proposal, solution, verification, and rejected work are charged.\\

cells & Only mathematically proved exclusions are removed before solving.\\
SDP backend & Remaining cells $\mapsto$ candidate witnesses or dual data &

\end{tabular}

The public decision has three outcomes.  \emph{Accept} means that all bad
cells are excluded by safe screening or externally checked certificates
and that the all-in cost inequality passes.  \emph{Reject} means that an
attainable bad cell has been witnessed, or that the verified ledger cannot
break even.  \emph{Uncertified} covers time limits, inaccurate statuses,
failed reconstruction, missing constants, or any uncovered cell.  This third
state is operationally important: it prevents numerical uncertainty from
being silently interpreted as safety.
Unsupported transcript syntax is treated the same way: the interface is
rejected before model construction and the continuation result is
\emph{uncertified}, not \emph{reject}.  The latter remains reserved for a
verified attainable bad cell or a verified ledger failure.
The reference implementation exposes preflight and aggregation as
\texttt{validate\_transcript\_interface},
\texttt{certified\_branch\_workflow}, and
\texttt{proof\_carrying\_gate}; unit tests cover pre-solver nonaffine failure,
complete exclusion,
verified counterexamples, missing cells, failed verification, duplicate cells,
and incomplete ledgers.

The reusable object is not the CVXPY model.  It is the tuple of (i) a semantic
parameter schema and exact named coefficient rows, (ii) a stopping-cell
enumerator, (iii) a consumer returning one verified status per bad cell, and
(iv) the solver-independent three-valued aggregator and ledger.  To add a new
stopping rule while retaining fixed-step gradient descent, a user supplies its
finite horizon and terminal/survival rows, emits the same cell-record schema,
and calls \texttt{certified\_branch\_workflow}; interpolation, exact arithmetic,
hashing, and aggregation are reused unchanged.  A new function class or
continuation map requires a new exact interpolation builder and cannot be
admitted by relabeling the old schema.  The artifact's
\texttt{REUSABILITY\_CONTRACT.md} gives a minimal extension and a fail-closed
nonaffine example.  This interface remains useful beyond $H=10$ even when the
current dense producer cannot find certificates: it preserves the decision and
verification semantics but makes no scalability claim.

\section{Joint transcript-conditioned cost certificate}
\label{sec:optimal-gate}

Define the scalar certificate
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma^*(\cT,y;c,m)
 :=\max\{\Delta^*(\cT,y)+m,\;c+\Delta^*(\cT,y)\}.
 \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Transcript-relative optimality of the exact certificate]
\label{thm:maximal-gate}
For a fixed transcript $\cT$, accepting $y$ satisfies both inequalities in
\eqref{eq:two-requirements} for every consistent function if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma^*(\cT,y;c,m)\leq0.                         \label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the rule \eqref{eq:accept} is maximal among deterministic gates
measurable with respect to the same data.  Any such gate that accepts when
$\Gamma^*>0$ fails on at least one consistent function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)$, set
$d_f=N_f(y)-N_f(x)$.  By definition, $d_f\leq\Delta^*$.  If
$\Gamma^*\leq0$, then $\Delta^*\leq-m$ and
$c+\Delta^*\leq0$.  Hence $d_f\leq-m$ and $c+d_f\leq0$, which are exactly
\eqref{eq:two-requirements} after rearrangement.

Conversely, suppose both inequalities hold for every consistent function.
Then $d_f\leq-m$ and $c+d_f\leq0$ for every $f$.  Taking suprema yields
$\Delta^*\leq-m$ and $c+\Delta^*\leq0$, so $\Gamma^*\leq0$.

Finally, if $\Gamma^*>0$, then either $\Delta^*>-m$ or
$\Delta^*>-c$.  By the definition of a supremum, in the first case there is a
consistent $f$ with $d_f>-m$; in the second there is one with $d_f>-c$.
That function violates the corresponding requirement.  A deterministic gate
seeing only the same transcript cannot distinguish it from the other
consistent functions.  Therefore no robust acceptance region can strictly
contain \eqref{eq:accept}.
\end{proof}

The final maximality statement is information relative and assumes exact
knowledge of the support value: strengthening the
transcript shrinks $\mathfrak F(\cT)$ and can only decrease $\Delta^*$.
Weakening the function class without evidence would make the certificate less
reliable.  The characterization motivates the software contract, whereas the
computational work is to evaluate it without losing joint dependence.
The implemented producer is not itself maximal: if even one required exact
cell exclusion is unavailable, it returns \emph{uncertified} although the
ideal rule may accept.

\begin{proposition}[Information monotonicity]
\label{prop:information}
If transcript $\cT'$ refines $\cT$ in the sense that
$\mathfrak F(\cT')\subseteq\mathfrak F(\cT)$ and both use the same candidate,
then $\Delta^*(\cT',y)\leq\Delta^*(\cT,y)$.  Thus, for fixed $c,m$, every
acceptance certified by $\cT$ remains certified by $\cT'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The supremum in \eqref{eq:delta} for $\cT'$ is taken over a subset of the
functions used for $\cT$.  The inequality follows, and \eqref{eq:gamma} is
nondecreasing in $\Delta^*$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why independent call bounds lose information}

Let
\[
 L_x:=\inf_{f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)}N_f(x),\qquad
 U_y:=\sup_{f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)}N_f(y).
\]
The rectangular certificate replaces $\Delta^*$ by
$\Delta_{\rm box}(\cT,y):=U_y-L_x$.

\begin{proposition}[Rectangle domination and an illustrative consistency class]
\label{prop:rectangle}
One always has
\begin{equation}
 \Delta^*\leq\Delta_{\rm box}.                     \label{eq:box-dominates}
\end{equation}
Consequently, rectangular acceptance implies joint acceptance.  The gap
$\Delta_{\rm box}-\Delta^*$ can nevertheless be arbitrarily large in an
abstract two-instance consistency class, even when both marginal bounds are
sharp.  This latter statement is illustrative; the class-internal
$\cF_{\mu,L}$ separation is proved separately below.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every jointly attainable pair $(r,s)$, $s\leq U_y$ and $r\geq L_x$;
hence $s-r\leq U_y-L_x$.  Taking the supremum proves
\eqref{eq:box-dominates}.

For the second claim, fix integers $K\geq d\geq0$ and $R\geq0$, and consider
the attainable set
\[
 \cC_R=\{(K,K-d),(K+R,K+R-d)\}.
\]
Both marginals are attained: $L_x=K$ and $U_y=K+R-d$.  Thus
$\Delta_{\rm box}=R-d$, whereas both paired differences equal $-d$, so
$\Delta^*=-d$.  Their gap is $R$, which is unbounded.
\end{proof}

The example is a two-instance consistency class.  Its point is not that every
abstract pair set arises from gradient descent; it shows precisely which
dependence the marginal relaxation discards.  The PEP construction below
computes that dependence for an optimization class.

\section{A finite horizon for gradient descent}
\label{sec:horizon}

The analysis now specializes to Euclidean fixed-step gradient descent
\begin{equation}
 z_{k+1}=z_k-\alpha\nabla f(z_k),\qquad
 N_f(z_0)=\min\{k\geq0:\norm{\nabla f(z_k)}\leq\eps\},
 \label{eq:gd}
\end{equation}
over the class $\cF_{\mu,L}$ of differentiable, $L$-smooth,
$\mu$-strongly convex functions, where $0<\mu<L<\infty$.  Let $x_*$ denote
the unique minimizer.  Throughout the finite-horizon and cell-SDP development
assume $0<\alpha<2/L$; this makes the gradient map contractive and the
stopping time finite under the distance bounds below.

\begin{remark}[$\mu=L$ boundary]
\label{rem:equal-curvature}
The strict inequality avoids the zero denominator in the interpolation formula
below; it is not an identifiability gap.  If $\mu=L$, strong monotonicity,
smoothness, and Cauchy--Schwarz give, for all $u,v$,
\[
 L\norm{u-v}^2
 \leq \ip{\nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)}{u-v}
 \leq \norm{\nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)}\norm{u-v}
 \leq L\norm{u-v}^2.
\]
Equality throughout implies $\nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)=L(u-v)$, so
$f(z)=\tfrac L2\norm{z}^2+a^\top z+b$.  Given any observed triple
$(z_i,f_i,g_i)$, set
\[
 x_*^{(i)}=z_i-g_i/L,
 \qquad
 b_*^{(i)}=f_i-\tfrac L2\norm{z_i-x_*^{(i)}}^2.
\]
The transcript is consistent if and only if these quantities agree across all
observations and the remaining declared constraints hold after substitution.
For either start $z_0$, the continuation is
\[
 z_k-x_*=(1-\alpha L)^k(z_0-x_*),\qquad
 \norm{\nabla f(z_k)}=L|1-\alpha L|^k\norm{z_0-x_*}.
\]
Consequently its stopping time is obtained without an SDP: it is zero when
$L\norm{z_0-x_*}\leq\eps$, it is one when $\alpha L=1$ and the initial
residual exceeds $\eps$, and otherwise it is
\[
 \left\lceil
 \frac{\log\!\bigl(\eps/(L\norm{z_0-x_*})\bigr)}
      {\log|1-\alpha L|}
 \right\rceil
\]
when the initial residual exceeds $\eps$.  Thus the boundary case has an
explicit consistency-and-stopping-time algorithm; the interpolation formula and the exact
cell-SDP results in \cref{thm:cell,thm:sdp-exact} are stated only for
$\mu<L$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[SDP-admissible transcript interface]
\label{def:transcript-interface}
An input contract is admitted by the implemented joint-cell backend only if
it certifies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{x-x_*}\leq R,\qquad \norm{y-x}\leq D,
 \label{eq:distance-bounds}
\end{equation}
and every additional relation has an exact finite representation affine in
sampled function values and Gram inner products.  An exact affine lifting is
also admissible when its equivalence to the original relation is separately
verified.  For every stopping cell, the retained relations must describe all
and only the sampled data of functions in
$\mathfrak F(\cT)\cap\cF_{\mu,L}$; no predicate may be silently dropped.
\end{definition}

A representative admissible cheap-oracle contract is
\begin{equation}
 \norm{y-x+\gamma\nabla f(x)}\leq\delta.            \label{eq:cheap-contract}
\end{equation}
A product constraint such as $f(z_1)f(z_2)=1$, or an unmodeled nonlinear
line-search predicate, fails the interface: neither is affine in the finite
function values and Gram entries without an additional exact lifting.  The
validator stops before SDP construction and returns \emph{uncertified}.  It
does not erase the relation, solve a relaxation, or label the proposal
\emph{reject}.  Thus this definition is an input specification, not a theorem
assumption about arbitrary user data.

Contract \eqref{eq:cheap-contract} is informative when the proposal is close
to the damped gradient step $x-\gamma\nabla f(x)$.  It does not, by itself,
certify an arbitrary Newton or quasi-Newton proposal.  Such a proposal enters
the present full-class theorem only if a separately verified residual
bound of the form \eqref{eq:cheap-contract} is available.  The modal, Krylov,
and active-face modules documented in the companion artifact are restricted
quadratic or local screeners, not substitutes for that full-class contract.

\begin{proposition}[Contract-radius monotonicity]
\label{prop:radius-monotonicity}
Fix every transcript field except the radius in
\eqref{eq:cheap-contract}, and write $\cT_\delta$ for the resulting transcript.
If $0\leq\delta_1\leq\delta_2$, then
\[
 \mathfrak F(\cT_{\delta_1})\subseteq
 \mathfrak F(\cT_{\delta_2}),\qquad
 \Gamma^*(\cT_{\delta_1},y;c,m)
 \leq \Gamma^*(\cT_{\delta_2},y;c,m).
\]
Thus weakening the gradient-step approximation can only preserve or destroy
acceptance; it cannot create a stronger certificate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The smaller residual ball is contained in the larger one.  The result follows
from \cref{prop:information} and the monotonicity of \eqref{eq:gamma} in
$\Delta^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified radius-error propagation]
\label{prop:radius-error}
Suppose a verified dual for cell $(r,s)$ at radius $\delta_0$ has upper bound
$u_{rs}<0$ and multiplier $\lambda_{rs}\geq0$ on the row
$\norm{y-x+\gamma g_x}^2\leq\delta_0^2$.  The same dual is valid at any
$\delta\geq\delta_0$ and has upper bound
\[
 u_{rs}+\lambda_{rs}(\delta^2-\delta_0^2).
\]
Consequently, a measured residual $\widehat\delta$ with certified absolute
error $e$ must be tested at $\delta=\widehat\delta+e$; acceptance remains valid
only if the displayed bound is negative for every bad cell.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only the right-hand side of the proposal-contract row changes.  Dual
stationarity, multiplier signs, and the positive semidefinite slack are
unchanged, while the dual objective changes by the stated amount.
\end{proof}

This rule makes deployment uncertainty explicit.  The original $\delta=1/100$
duals remain strict only through radius $0.0113158\ldots$; beyond that value a
new recovery is needed.  A separate complete exact suite at
$\delta=\HsixMediumRadius{}$ verifies \HsixMediumCertificates{} cells and
\HsixMediumPivots{} positive pivots, with largest upper bound
\HsixMediumUpper{}$<0$.  The critical $(1,1)$ floating SDP is already positive
at $\delta=2/125$, and at $1/50$ it is approximately $7.41\times10^{-4}$.
Thus the present envelope cannot certify those radii; the correct outcome is
\emph{uncertified}, not an extrapolated acceptance.  The older $H=3$ sweep in
the artifact provides a wider diagnostic but no exact exclusion claim.

If the current exact gradient is not already in the transcript, contract
\eqref{eq:cheap-contract} does not reveal it; the inequality only restricts
every exact gradient consistent with the proposal.  If that gradient was
computed before the branch point and is therefore sunk on both paths, the
following construction gives all displayed transcript bounds without another
exact call.

\begin{proposition}[A zero-new-call transcript pipeline]
\label{prop:transcript-pipeline}
Suppose a certified class constant $\mu>0$ and a previously observed gradient
$g_x=\nabla f(x)$ belong to the common transcript.  After constructing $y$, set
\begin{equation}
 R=\frac{\norm{g_x}}{\mu},\qquad
 D=\norm{y-x},\qquad
 \delta=\norm{y-x+\gamma g_x}.                    \label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:distance-bounds}--\eqref{eq:cheap-contract} hold for every
$f\in\cF_{\mu,L}$ consistent with the transcript.  Computing
\eqref{eq:pipeline} requires no new exact-oracle evaluation; proposal and
arithmetic costs remain part of $c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Strong monotonicity of the gradient and $\nabla f(x_*)=0$ give
\[
 \mu\norm{x-x_*}^2
 \leq\ip{g_x}{x-x_*}
 \leq\norm{g_x}\norm{x-x_*},
\]
so $\norm{x-x_*}\leq\norm{g_x}/\mu$.  The definitions of $D$ and
$\delta$ make the other two inequalities identities.  Only stored data, the
candidate, and vector arithmetic are used.
\end{proof}

The proposition does not make $\mu$ free.  It must come from problem structure
or a separately certified enclosure and its acquisition cost must be charged.
Moreover, $R=\norm{g_x}/\mu$ is the sharp worst-case distance guarantee
available from only strong monotonicity and the observed gradient; it need not
be a tight estimate for the realized function.
For example, if a Hessian enclosure on the relevant domain satisfies
$H_{ii}\in[\ell_{ii},u_{ii}]$ and $|H_{ij}|\leq b_{ij}$ for $i\neq j$, then
Gershgorin bounds give the verifiable choices
\begin{equation}
 \mu=\min_i\left(\ell_{ii}-\sum_{j\neq i}b_{ij}\right),\qquad
 L=\max_i\left(u_{ii}+\sum_{j\neq i}b_{ij}\right),                 \label{eq:gershgorin}
\end{equation}
provided the first value is positive.  Entrywise interval enclosures can be
far cheaper than an exact solve in structured problems, but producing them is
application dependent; \eqref{eq:gershgorin} is a verifiable route, not a
universal claim of low cost.

The auxiliary constructive modules in the artifact connect to the joint-cell
engine through this interface, not through an additional theorem assumption.  A
matrix, Krylov, active-face, or active-manifold screener may append any
certified $R,D,\delta$ inequalities to $\cT$ and must append its online cost to
$c$.  By \cref{prop:information}, valid extra inequalities can only strengthen
the joint certificate.  If a module returns only separate call-count bounds,
it implements the rectangular relaxation in \cref{prop:rectangle}; it is a
safe preliminary screen, not the joint PEP certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform finite horizon]
\label{prop:horizon}
Let $0<\alpha<2/L$ and
\[
 q:=\max\{|1-\alpha\mu|,|1-\alpha L|\}<1,
 \qquad D_0:=\max\{R,R+D\}.
\]
If $LD_0>\eps$, define
\begin{equation}
 H:=\left\lceil\frac{\log(\eps/(LD_0))}{\log q}\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:horizon}
\end{equation}
If $LD_0\leq\eps$, take $H=0$.  Then
$N_f(x),N_f(y)\leq H$ for every $f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)\cap\cF_{\mu,L}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $f\in\cF_{\mu,L}$, the gradient-descent operator
$T_\alpha=I-\alpha\nabla f$ in \eqref{eq:gd} is $q$-Lipschitz.  To justify
this without assuming a classical Hessian everywhere, restrict $\nabla f$ to
the segment from $u$ to $v$.  Lipschitz continuity makes that restriction
absolutely continuous.  Rademacher's theorem gives a derivative almost
  everywhere; the a.e. second differentiability of $C^{1,1}$ functions implies
  that this derivative is symmetric almost everywhere
  \citep[Sec.~6.3]{EvansGariepy2015}, and strong monotonicity and
$L$-Lipschitz continuity give eigenvalues in $[\mu,L]$.  Denote this
almost-everywhere derivative by $\mathcal H$.  Therefore
\[
 \nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)=\bar{\mathcal H}(u-v),\qquad
 \bar{\mathcal H}:=\int_0^1 \mathcal H(v+t(u-v))\,dt,
\]
where $\mu I\preceq\bar{\mathcal H}\preceq LI$.  Spectral calculus yields
$\norm{I-\alpha\bar{\mathcal H}}\leq q$, hence
$\norm{T_\alpha u-T_\alpha v}\leq q\norm{u-v}$.  Because
$x_*$ is fixed by the map,
\[
 \norm{z_k-x_*}\leq q^k\norm{z_0-x_*},
 \qquad
 \norm{\nabla f(z_k)}\leq Lq^k\norm{z_0-x_*}.
\]
The first initial distance is at most $R$; the second is at most $R+D$ by the
triangle inequality.  Thus both gradient norms are at most $LD_0q^k$.
The definition of $H$ makes this quantity no larger than $\eps$ at $k=H$.
\end{proof}

This proposition turns the infinite stopping-time comparison into at most
$(H+1)^2$ discrete cells.  Sharper transcript-specific horizons can replace
\eqref{eq:horizon} without changing the results below.

\subsection{The marginal loss is unbounded inside the smooth strongly convex class}

The abstract pair-set example in \cref{prop:rectangle} is not responsible for
the unbounded gap.  It persists under the same smooth strongly convex dynamics
used in the rest of the paper.

\begin{theorem}[Class-internal unbounded separation]
\label{thm:class-gap}
Fix $0<\mu<L$, $0<\alpha<1/L$, and $G>0$.  For each
$\eps\in(0,G)$, let $\cT_\eps$ record the one-dimensional data
\begin{equation}
 x=0,\quad f(x)=0,\quad \nabla f(x)=G,\quad
 y=-\alpha G,\quad R=G/\mu,\quad D=\alpha G,          \label{eq:gap-transcript}
\end{equation}
and the class $\cF_{\mu,L}$.  Then the fixed-step method \eqref{eq:gd} obeys
\begin{equation}
 \Delta^*(\cT_\eps,y)=-1,\qquad
 \Delta_{\rm box}(\cT_\eps,y)-\Delta^*(\cT_\eps,y)
 \longrightarrow\infty\quad\text{as }\eps\downarrow0.            \label{eq:class-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus the rectangular relaxation can lose arbitrarily many certified calls
even for one-dimensional quadratics sharing the complete current first-order
transcript and a common distance bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $y=x-\alpha\nabla f(x)$ and $G>\eps$, the trajectory from $y$ is the
trajectory from $x$ shifted by exactly one iteration for every consistent
function.  Hence $N_f(y)=N_f(x)-1$ and $\Delta^*=-1$.

For $\lambda\in\{\mu,L\}$, consider the two transcript-consistent witnesses
\begin{equation}
 f_\lambda(t)=\frac{\lambda}{2}t^2+Gt.               \label{eq:gap-witness}
\end{equation}
They satisfy $f_\lambda(0)=0$, $f_\lambda'(0)=G$, and
$|x-x_\lambda^*|=G/\lambda\leq G/\mu$; both belong to
$\cF_{\mu,L}$.  Their gradient residuals are
$G(1-\alpha\lambda)^k$, so
\begin{equation}
 N_\lambda(x)=
 \left\lceil
 \frac{\log(G/\eps)}{-\log(1-\alpha\lambda)}
 \right\rceil.                                      \label{eq:gap-count}
\end{equation}
Since $1-\alpha\mu>1-\alpha L>0$, the difference
$N_\mu(x)-N_L(x)$ diverges linearly in $\log(G/\eps)$.  The marginal range
over the full consistency class is at least this two-witness difference.
Using $N_f(y)=N_f(x)-1$,
\[
 \Delta_{\rm box}-\Delta^*
 =\sup_f N_f(x)-\inf_f N_f(x)
 \geq N_\mu(x)-N_L(x)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Exact stopping-cell construction}
\label{sec:pep}

For $r,s\in\{0,\ldots,H\}$, define two trajectories
\begin{align}
 x_0&=x,&x_{k+1}&=x_k-\alpha g_k^x,&g_k^x&=\nabla f(x_k),\label{eq:xtraj}\\
 y_0&=y,&y_{k+1}&=y_k-\alpha g_k^y,&g_k^y&=\nabla f(y_k).\label{eq:ytraj}
\end{align}
Cell $(r,s)$ requires
\begin{align}
 \norm{g_r^x}^2&\leq\eps^2,&
 \norm{g_k^x}^2&>\eps^2&& (0\leq k<r),\label{eq:xstop}\\
 \norm{g_s^y}^2&\leq\eps^2,&
 \norm{g_k^y}^2&>\eps^2&& (0\leq k<s).\label{eq:ystop}
\end{align}
The strict inequalities matter: replacing them by weak inequalities adds
boundary functions whose actual stopping time is earlier.

For a finite list $(z_i,f_i,g_i)$, smooth strongly convex interpolation is
equivalent to the pairwise inequalities
\begin{align}
 f_i-f_j-\ip{g_j}{z_i-z_j}
 \geq \frac{1}{2(1-\mu/L)}\bigg(
 &\frac1L\norm{g_i-g_j}^2+\mu\norm{z_i-z_j}^2 \nonumber\\[-1mm]
 &-\frac{2\mu}{L}\ip{g_i-g_j}{z_i-z_j}\bigg),
 \label{eq:interpolation}
\end{align}
for every $i,j$ \citep{TaylorInterpolation2017}.  The construction includes all points and
gradients from both trajectories and $(x_*,f_*,0)$ in this list, together with
any exact anchors already in $\cT$.

To encode strict survival, introduce one common scalar margin $\tau$ and
replace every strict inequality in \eqref{eq:xstop}--\eqref{eq:ystop} by
\begin{equation}
 \norm{g_k^a}^2\geq\eps^2+\tau,\qquad a\in\{x,y\}.
 \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
Let $\tau_{rs}^*$ be the supremum of $\tau$ subject to
\eqref{eq:xtraj}--\eqref{eq:ytraj}, \eqref{eq:interpolation}, the terminal
weak inequalities, \eqref{eq:margin}, and every transcript constraint.  If
$r=s=0$, there are no strict constraints; that one cell uses ordinary
feasibility rather than maximizing $\tau$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact cell test]
\label{thm:cell}
For a transcript admitted by \cref{def:transcript-interface}, and for
$(r,s)\neq(0,0)$,
there exists a consistent function with
$(N_f(x),N_f(y))=(r,s)$ if and only if $\tau_{rs}^*>0$.  Cell $(0,0)$ is
attainable if and only if its weak feasibility problem is feasible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that a consistent function has stopping pair $(r,s)$.  The list of
pre-terminal gradient norms is finite and every one is strictly larger than
$\eps$.  Hence
\[
 \bar\tau:=\min\{\norm{g_k^a}^2-\eps^2:
 a=x,\ 0\leq k<r\text{ or }a=y,\ 0\leq k<s\}>0.
\]
Its sampled data satisfy all PEP constraints with $\tau=\bar\tau$, so
$\tau_{rs}^*>0$.

Conversely, if $\tau_{rs}^*>0$, choose any
$0<\eta<\tau_{rs}^*$.  By the definition of supremum, the feasible set
contains a point with $\tau>\eta>0$.  The interpolation
theorem produces an $f\in\cF_{\mu,L}$ realizing all sampled triples.
The recurrence constraints make the sampled points its two gradient-descent
trajectories.  Equation \eqref{eq:margin} gives strict nontermination before
$r$ and $s$, while the terminal constraints give termination at those indices.
The transcript constraints make the same function consistent with $\cT$.
Thus its stopping pair is exactly $(r,s)$.  When $r=s=0$, only the two terminal
conditions are needed, which proves the final statement.
\end{proof}

There is no missing boundary cell when a gradient norm equals $\eps$.  By the
stopping convention, equality terminates at that index and is assigned to the
corresponding earlier-terminal cell.  A later-terminal cell requires strict
survival there and therefore cannot contain the same function.  Conversely,
every true later-terminal cell has only finitely many preterminal norms, all
strictly above $\eps$, so the minimum margin used in the proof is positive.
Thus a zero margin in an incompatible cell is not interpreted as attainment.

\begin{lemma}[Prefix or ragged equivalence]
\label{lem:ragged}
For a fixed cell $(r,s)$, imposing interpolation only on the two prefixes
$x_0,\ldots,x_r$ and $y_0,\ldots,y_s$ gives the same cell test as a padded
horizon formulation, provided neither formulation adds conditions on
post-terminal iterates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Any full feasible trajectory restricts to feasible prefixes.  Conversely, the
interpolation theorem applied to feasible prefixes constructs one consistent
function realizing them.  Gradient descent on that function uniquely extends
both prefixes after $r$ and $s$; those later iterates do not enter the stopping
event or transcript.  Thus padding adds variables but no restriction relevant
to the cell.
\end{proof}

\section{Dimension-free SDP backend and realizability}
\label{sec:sdp}

Choose vector atoms containing every sampled gradient, $x_*$, $y-x$, and any
additional transcript vectors.  Set the origin at $x$.  Recurrences
\eqref{eq:xtraj}--\eqref{eq:ytraj} express every sampled point as a fixed linear
combination of these atoms.  Let $P$ collect their coordinates and let
\begin{equation}
 G=P^\top P\succeq0                                      \label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
be the Gram matrix.  Every inner product in
\eqref{eq:interpolation}, the stopping constraints, distance bounds, and the
representative cheap contract \eqref{eq:cheap-contract} is affine in $G$.
Function values and $\tau$ enter linearly.  Therefore each cell is an SDP.

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-free exactness and fixed-dimension qualification]
\label{thm:sdp-exact}
Let $p$ be the number of vector atoms in a joint cell.  The Gram SDP described
above has the same value $\tau_{rs}^*$ as the joint PEP over Hilbert spaces, and
over $\R^d$ whenever $d\geq p$.  More precisely, every feasible SDP point is
realizable in dimension $\rank(G)$.  For a prescribed dimension $d<p$, exact
equivalence is recovered by adding $\rank(G)\leq d$; without this nonconvex
rank constraint, the SDP is an outer problem and hence gives a safe upper
certificate for exclusion.
\end{theorem}

Thus ``dimension-free'' has a strong operational meaning: one verified outer
SDP exclusion contains no physical dimension parameter and rules out the cell
simultaneously in every finite dimension.  A feasible witness of rank $r_G$ is
realizable in every $d\geq r_G$; only using that witness to reject in a
prespecified smaller dimension requires the additional rank qualification.

\begin{proof}
Given any consistent function and its two sampled trajectories, form $P$ from
the actual vectors.  Its matrix $G=P^\top P$ is positive semidefinite and
reproduces every inner product, so all SDP constraints hold with the same
margin.  Thus the SDP is no more restrictive than the functional PEP.

Conversely, factor any feasible $G\succeq0$ as $G=P^\top P$ with
$P\in\R^{r_G\times p}$ and $r_G=\rank(G)$.  Interpret the columns of $P$ as
the vector atoms.  Their linear combinations reproduce all point and gradient
inner products appearing in the SDP.  The pairwise inequalities
\eqref{eq:interpolation} are necessary and sufficient, so the smooth strongly
convex interpolation theorem constructs a function on $\R^{r_G}$ with exactly
those sampled values and gradients.  The linear recurrences and transcript
relations are therefore realized, with the same $\tau$.  Embedding
$\R^{r_G}$ isometrically into any $\R^d$ with $d\geq r_G$ changes nothing.
This proves equality when dimension is unrestricted or $d\geq p$.

If the ambient dimension is prescribed below $p$, an actual sample necessarily
has $\rank(G)\leq d$, and the same factorization proves sufficiency of that
condition.  Dropping it enlarges the feasible set, so an SDP proof that the
enlarged cell has no positive margin remains valid for the fixed-dimensional
subclass.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three-point reduction for a terminal marginal]
\label{lem:three-point-marginal}
Suppose a transcript contains only exact affine Gram/function-value relations
among $x$, $y$, and $x_*$, and the marginal objective is
$\norm{\nabla f(y)}^2$.  Then its dimension-free worst case over
$\cF_{\mu,L}$ equals the Gram SDP containing precisely the three sampled
triples $(x,f_x,g_x)$, $(y,f_y,g_y)$, and $(x_*,f_*,0)$, with all ordered
interpolation inequalities and the transcript rows.  No unobserved continuation
point is needed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every consistent function supplies a feasible three-triple interpolation
record with the same objective.  Conversely, the necessary-and-sufficient
interpolation theorem \citep[Thm.~4]{TaylorInterpolation2017} realizes every
feasible record by a function in $\cF_{\mu,L}$, and the transcript relations
are among those same triples.  The two suprema therefore coincide.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the main reason for using exact interpolation rather than a
collection of ad hoc inequalities: a positive-margin SDP point is not merely a
relaxation artifact in the dimension-free class.  It reconstructs an
adversarial function.  The same cell disjunction applies to other stopping
rules whenever terminal and strict-survival conditions remain affine in
function values and Gram entries.  In particular, function-gap stopping
$f(z_k)-f_*\leq\eps$ and distance stopping
$\norm{z_k-x_*}^2\leq\eps^2$ fit after replacing the gradient-norm rows;
nonquadratic or history-dependent tests require a new exact lifting.

For a prescribed $d<p$, the software semantics are necessarily asymmetric.
An exclusion of the outer SDP remains a valid \emph{accept} proof.  A positive
outer-SDP witness is a fixed-$d$ \emph{reject} proof only after exact
verification of $\rank(G)\leq d$ or reconstruction of explicit coordinates
in $\R^d$.  A future verifier could establish rank by rational row reduction,
equivalently by vanishing and nonvanishing exact minors; the current consumer
does not implement this route, and floating singular values are not proof
objects.  A higher-rank positive witness therefore leaves
the fixed-$d$ decision uncertified.  The rank-constrained fixed-dimensional
gate is exact but nonconvex, while the implemented outer gate is complete only
for exclusion.

\section{Three-valued certification algorithm}
\label{sec:bad-cells}

Within the horizon, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B(c,m):=\{(r,s):s>r-m\ \text{or}\ c+s>r\}.   \label{eq:bad}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:two-requirements}, these are exactly the stopping pairs that
violate at least one desired inequality.  No rounding is implicit: because
$r,s$ are integers, $c+s>r$ is equivalent to
$s\geq r+1-\lceil c\rceil$ for $c>0$ (and to $s>r$ for $c=0$).  In the
reported case $m=1$ and $0<c\leq1$, either disjunct gives $s\geq r$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite bad-cell acceptance test]
\label{thm:finite-gate}
For a transcript admitted by \cref{def:transcript-interface}, with
$0<\alpha<2/L$ and the exact interpolation model of \cref{thm:cell}, a
candidate is robustly acceptable if and only if
every cell in $\mathcal B(c,m)$ is excluded: for $(r,s)\neq(0,0)$,
$\tau_{rs}^*\leq0$ or the SDP is infeasible, and for $(0,0)$ the feasibility
problem is infeasible.  Solving only these bad cells decides robust acceptance;
solving all cells additionally recovers $\Delta^*$ and hence $\Gamma^*$ in
\eqref{eq:gamma}.  With exact cell decisions, the resulting finite SDP rule is
transcript-relative optimal among deterministic rules using the same
transcript and class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:horizon}, every jointly attainable stopping pair lies in the
finite grid.  By \cref{thm:cell,thm:sdp-exact}, a grid cell is attainable
exactly when its PEP has positive strict margin (with the stated exception for
$(0,0)$).  If all cells in $\mathcal B(c,m)$ are excluded, every attainable
pair obeys both inequalities in \eqref{eq:two-requirements}, so acceptance is
safe.

If some bad cell has positive margin, SDP exactness constructs a consistent
function whose stopping pair is that cell.  It violates at least one required
inequality, so acceptance is unsafe.  Thus the test is necessary and
sufficient for acceptance.  Conversely, if $\Gamma^*>0$, the definition of a
supremum gives a consistent function with a positive violation (or an
arbitrarily close one that is still positive); its stopping pair belongs to
the finite grid and hence to a bad cell.  By \cref{thm:cell}, that cell has
positive strict margin, with the stated $(0,0)$ feasibility convention.  If
all cells are solved, maximizing $s-r$ over
the attainable set gives $\Delta^*$ and therefore \eqref{eq:gamma}; maximality
then follows from \cref{thm:maximal-gate}.  Solving only the bad cells does not
by itself return the numerical value of $\Delta^*$.
\end{proof}

Only bad cells must be solved.  A positive-margin bad cell immediately rejects
the proposal in the dimension-free semantics.  For a prescribed dimension
$d<p$, the present implementation does not claim a rejection: it returns
\emph{uncertified} unless an external exact rank or coordinate witness is
supplied.  No fixed-$d$ rejection experiment is reported.  Acceptance remains
safe without the rank constraint because the SDP is an outer problem.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: three-valued C2OGate decision.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item validate the interface of \cref{def:transcript-interface}.  If a
  relation is nonaffine, has inexact coefficients, or invokes an unverified
  lifting, stop before invoking a solver and return \emph{uncertified};
  otherwise validate the constants and compute the finite horizon;
  \item create $\mathcal B(c,m)$ and remove only cells covered by exact
  structural or analytic screeners;
  \item solve the remaining cells, stopping with \emph{reject} after any
  exactly validated positive-margin witness;
  \item reconstruct and externally check an exclusion proof for every
  unresolved bad cell; and
  \item return \emph{accept} only if no cell is left uncertified and the full
  ledger satisfies \eqref{eq:two-requirements}; otherwise return
  \emph{uncertified}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}
This asymmetry makes rejection easier than acceptance and ensures that solver
failure cannot improve the declared outcome.  The algorithm terminates after a
finite cell list and a finite recovery grid, but it is deliberately incomplete:
unsupported transcript syntax, a solver time limit, failed rational recovery,
a signed-margin boundary cell, or a missing ledger item all produce
\emph{uncertified} rather than a guessed decision.

\section{Externally checkable exclusion verification}
\label{sec:dual}

A floating-point solver status is not a mathematical proof of cell exclusion.
The SDP formulation supports externally checkable conic certificates.  In
generic conic form, a margin cell is
\begin{equation}
 \sup_X\{\ip{C}{X}:\mathcal A X=b,\ \mathcal D X\leq h,\ X\in K\},
 \label{eq:conic-primal}
\end{equation}
where $X$ collects $(G,f_i,\tau)$, $K$ contains the positive-semidefinite cone,
and $\ip{C}{X}=\tau$.  A dual feasible triple $(u,v,Z)$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A^*u+\mathcal D^*v-C=Z\in K^*,\qquad v\geq0,
 \label{eq:dual-identity}
\end{equation}
and upper-bounds \eqref{eq:conic-primal} by $b^\top u+h^\top v$.
The entries of $v$ are nonnegative weights on interpolation, transcript, and
stopping inequalities; $Z$ is a positive-semidefinite remainder.

\paragraph{Consumer-side model reconstruction.}
The certificate payload does not supply authoritative primal coefficient matrices.
For each declared parameter tuple and cell label, the standard-library
consumer rebuilds the vector atoms, the two affine
gradient-descent trajectories, every ordered smooth strongly convex
interpolation inequality, the distance and proposal-contract rows, the
terminal and strict-survival stopping rows, the margin row, the value anchor,
and the redundant trace bound.  All coefficients and right-hand sides are
formed with \texttt{fractions.Fraction}; no floating-point coefficient emitted
by CVXPY, Clarabel, SCS, or the recovery producer is read as evidence.  Unknown
multiplier names, incorrect row counts, or a parameter mismatch are rejected.
Only after this reconstruction does the consumer form stationarity, the dual
objective, and the slack matrix and check them exactly.  Thus the
producer-side modeling and solver layers are outside the trusted acceptance
boundary; the trusted kernel includes the smaller, separately implemented
semantic-to-coefficient reconstruction and exact rational algebra.  This is a
machine check of the instantiated finite SDP, not a formalization of the
smooth strongly convex interpolation theorem proved in the paper.

\ConsumerFuzzCases{} randomized legal rational tuples compare the CVXPY
producer and nonsharing SymPy builder in \ConsumerFuzzComparisons{} exact
scalar coefficients without a mismatch.  PEPit 0.5.1 also rebuilds
\PaddedCrosscheckCells{} padded cells numerically, with maximum objective

implementation and would expose row-level errors; they do not formalize the
interpolation theorem or prove either program correct.

\paragraph{Implemented rational recovery.}
For each cell, the artifact applies the following heuristic success-or-fail
procedure to a numerical dual.  There is no completeness, termination, or
recovery-success theorem; the mathematical guarantee begins only after a
candidate certificate has been reconstructed.  The producer (i) removes tiny
entries and reconstructs the others as
fractions with denominator at most $10^6$; (ii) gives a proved redundant trace
constraint a small positive multiplier, creating strict slack; (iii) forms the
exact stationarity system for function values and the margin; and (iv) corrects
a full-row-rank subset over $\mathbb Q$.  The $H=10$ generator tries a frozen
finite list of activity thresholds and two trace regularizers, all outside the
trust boundary.  A candidate is retained only if every inequality multiplier
is nonnegative, stationarity is exact, the rational objective is negative,
and exact unpivoted $LDL^\top$ elimination has only positive pivots.  Any
failed condition returns \emph{uncertified}.  Thus the numerical choices affect
recovery success, not the validity of a recovered certificate; unlike
recomputing all leading determinants, the $LDL^\top$ check reuses elimination
work and controls rational-expression growth.  This narrow rounding pipeline
is not presented as a replacement for exact algebraic LMI algorithms under
genericity assumptions \citep{HenrionNaldiSafeyElDin2016}, structured exact
rounding \citep{DostertDeLaatMoustrou2021}, or hybrid symbolic--numeric and
facial-reduction methods for degenerate SDPs
\citep{KolmogorovNaldiZapata2025}.  Those methods enlarge the set of instances
on which a producer may succeed; C2OGate's contribution is the domain-specific
consumer and all-cells decision rule once a supported certificate exists.
For reproducibility, the implemented producer has the following
pseudocode-level input/output and failure contract.

\paragraph{Algorithm 2 (finite-grid rational recovery producer).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Input:} an exact parameter tuple, one bad-cell index, an
  ordered finite grid of activity thresholds and trace regularizers, a
  denominator cap, and a numerical primal--dual solution.
  \item For each grid point, zero numerical multipliers below its threshold,
  recover the remaining entries with the denominator cap, and insert the
  selected positive trace multiplier.
  \item Rebuild the exact stationarity equations.  Select an exact
  full-row-rank correction subset, solve it over $\mathbb Q$, and update only
  that subset.  Skip the grid point if no such subset exists or the rational
  system is inconsistent.
  \item Reject the candidate if a multiplier is negative, stationarity or the
  dual objective identity is nonzero, the required objective sign fails, or
  any exact $LDL^\top$ pivot is nonpositive.
  \item \textbf{Output:} the first passing rational dual together with the
  attempted-grid prefix and exact row names; if the finite grid is exhausted,
  output \emph{recovery failure}.  The public gate maps that failure to
  \emph{uncertified}.
\end{enumerate}
This is a deterministic specification once the frozen numerical solution and
grid are supplied; it is not a convergence theorem.  The producer records the
ordered threshold--regularizer prefix attempted for each cell.  If only $k$ of
$n$ bad cells receive externally checkable exclusions, the public result
is \emph{uncertified} with progress $k/n$; partial coverage is never promoted
to partial acceptance.  The design follows the verified-rounding philosophy
in \citep{PeyrlParrilo2008,KaltofenLiYangZhi2008}; degenerate-face failure is
handled conservatively as in \citep{MonniauxCorbineau2011}.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional certificate routes]
\label{prop:dual-routes}
Suppose a feasible bad-cell SDP has finite optimal value at most zero and relative
Slater regularity is established on its minimal cone face.  Then it admits a
dual solution with objective at most zero.  A strongly infeasible cell admits a separating conic
alternative.  If instead an explicit extended dual or facial-reduction
sequence is supplied for a weakly infeasible or degenerate cell, that object
can be checked as its exclusion certificate.  These are conditional
certificate routes, not an assertion that the implementation automatically
detects the applicable regularity case.  The implementation does not decide
regularity: it parses only a supplied proof object and verifies its algebraic
conditions.  If no supported object verifies, the cell remains
\emph{uncertified}, so the gate fails closed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Relative Slater regularity gives conic strong duality and attainment for a
feasible finite-dimensional SDP.  A cell with primal optimum at most zero
therefore has a dual feasible point with the same nonpositive objective; weak
duality alone makes that point a verifiable exclusion certificate for the
\emph{strict-survival} margin of \cref{thm:cell}, where attainability requires
a strictly positive optimum.  The signed-margin implementation below has a
different boundary convention: a value of zero may be attainable, so its
consumer requires a strictly negative dual objective.  Strong
infeasibility is precisely strict separation from the affine constraint set,
which gives a conic alternative.  Semidefinite systems can also be weakly
infeasible, so an ordinary Farkas ray must not be asserted in that case.
Facial reduction identifies the minimal face for feasible degenerate systems
\citep{BorweinWolkowicz1981}, while exact extended SDP duality covers the
remaining pathologies \citep{Ramana1997}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Redundant padded trace and margin bounds]
\label{lem:trace-bound}
Suppose $0<\alpha<2/L$, $\norm{x-x_*}\leq R$, and
$\norm{y-x}\leq D$.  In the padded horizon-$H$ construction whose atoms are
$H+1$ gradients from each trajectory, the minimizer displacement, and the
proposal displacement, every feasible cell satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{tr}G
 \leq (H+1)L^2\{R^2+(R+D)^2\}+R^2+D^2.             \label{eq:trace-bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover, if $\eps\leq L(R+D)$, adjoining
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\tau\leq L^2(R+D)^2                       \label{eq:margin-upper}
\end{equation}
to the signed-margin formulation does not change its feasible set.  In
particular, the right side of \eqref{eq:trace-bound} is less than $27$, $49$,
and $66$ for the padded $H=3$ cross-check, the natural-$H=10$ stress suite,
and the natural-$H=6$ unified flagship, respectively.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:sdp-exact}, each feasible Gram/function-value point admits a
realization by one function in $\cF_{\mu,L}$ (in the dimension-free
semantics), so the trajectory bounds may be applied to that realization.
The gradient map is contractive and hence nonexpansive.  Every padded
baseline point remains within $R$ of $x_*$ and every padded candidate point
within $R+D$.  Smoothness gives
$\norm{\nabla f(z)}\leq L\norm{z-x_*}$, so the $2(H+1)$ gradient atoms
contribute the first term in \eqref{eq:trace-bound}; the two displacement
atoms contribute $R^2+D^2$.  The terminal signed row gives
$\widehat\tau\leq\eps^2\leq L^2(R+D)^2$, proving
\eqref{eq:margin-upper} redundant.
Postterminal iterates are the unique continuation of the interpolated
prefixes and carry no additional stopping constraint, so the same norm bounds
hold without changing the prefix-cell semantics.  Direct substitution gives
\[
4(6/5)^2+4(217/100)^2+(6/5)^2+(97/100)^2<27,
\]
$11+11(181/100)^2+1+(81/100)^2<49$, and
$7(9/5)^2+7(59/25)^2+(9/5)^2+(14/25)^2<66$.
\end{proof}

There is no circular use of the trace inequality in the exactness theorem.
First, \cref{thm:sdp-exact} realizes every point of the \emph{unpadded,
unbounded} cell SDP.  Applying the trajectory contraction to that realized
function proves \eqref{eq:trace-bound}; only then is the redundant row adjoined
to help the dual producer.  Removing the row recovers the original exact cell
semantics.

\paragraph{Signed cell margin used in the experiments.}
For a padded cell $(r,s)$, the producer replaces the two terminal inequalities
by
\begin{equation}
 \norm{g_r^x}^2+\widehat\tau\leq\eps^2,
 \qquad
 \norm{g_s^y}^2+\widehat\tau\leq\eps^2,              \label{eq:signed-terminal}
\end{equation}
and uses \eqref{eq:margin} with $\tau=\widehat\tau$ before each terminal.
Thus $\tau=\widehat\tau$ in every signed experimental model; the hat only
distinguishes the two-sided signed convention from the survival-only margin of
\cref{thm:cell}.
Attainability implies $\widehat\tau_{rs}^*\geq0$; an externally checked
negative dual upper bound therefore excludes the cell, including $(0,0)$.
This is the common computational object used by the natural-$H=6$ unified
flagship, nonzero-radius ten-cell regression, and natural-$H=10$ stress suite.
Their cell
counts, rational objectives, and exact replay records are reported there as
computational results rather than propositions.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness boundary of the signed-dual consumer]
\label{prop:signed-boundary}
The implemented signed-margin consumer can exclude a cell only from a dual
feasible point with objective strictly below zero.  Consequently, if
$\widehat\tau_{rs}^*=0$, no ordinary dual satisfying this sign convention can
certify exclusion.  Such a cell remains \emph{uncertified} unless a separately
implemented infeasibility, facial-reduction, or extended-dual proof is
provided.  This limitation affects completeness, not soundness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Weak duality makes every feasible dual objective an upper bound on
$\widehat\tau_{rs}^*$.  A strictly negative upper bound is impossible when the
primal optimum equals zero.  Returning \emph{uncertified} therefore cannot
create a false acceptance.  Facial reduction and Ramana's extended dual
\citep{BorweinWolkowicz1981,Ramana1997} are possible proof formats, but the
current consumer does not parse them.
\end{proof}

A cross-suite audit covers \SignedBoundaryCells{} signed cells from five
distinct $H=3$, $H=6$, and $H=10$ transcripts.  Exactly
\SignedBoundaryExactZero{} have a verified rational upper bound equal to zero,
and \SignedBoundaryFloatingNearZero{} lie within $10^{-8}$ of zero even before
rational recovery.  The UCI and rolling deployments reuse a strict H6 suite:
among their \SignedBoundaryWorkloadAccepted{} accepted transcripts,
\SignedBoundaryWorkloadZero{} depend on a zero-boundary exclusion.  These are
observed frequencies, not a distribution-free claim that boundary cells are
rare.

\begin{lemma}[Homogeneous transport of a verified cell dual]
\label{lem:homogeneous-transport}
Fix a signed-margin cell model whose transcript relations are homogeneous under
the positive scaling
\[
 (z,g,R,D,\delta,\eps)\mapsto
 (az,ag,aR,aD,a\delta,a\eps),\qquad
 (f,\widehat\tau,B_{\rm tr})\mapsto
 (a^2f,a^2\widehat\tau,a^2B_{\rm tr}),
\]
where $a>0$ and $B_{\rm tr}$ is the redundant trace bound.  If a rational dual
is feasible with objective $u<0$ for the source cell, the same multipliers and
slack matrix are dual feasible for the scaled cell with objective $a^2u<0$.
Thus the exclusion transports without a second numerical recovery.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After the displayed change of primal variables, every interpolation,
transcript, terminal, survival, trace, and margin-upper relation has the same
coefficient row and a right-hand side multiplied by $a^2$.  Hence the dual
stationarity identity and positive-semidefinite slack are unchanged, while the
dual objective is multiplied by $a^2$.  Positivity of $a$ preserves its strict
sign.  The activity threshold and trace regularizer used to \emph{discover} the
source dual are not transported assumptions: once the rational source dual has
verified, only its exact multipliers enter this argument.
\end{proof}

At the smaller padded $H=3$ cross-check, the ten cell models share
substantially more than their dimensions.  Their 72
ordered interpolation inequalities, six proposal/distance/trace/margin
inequalities, and one anchor equality are coefficient-identical.  Only the two
terminal rows and zero to six survival rows select different trajectory
indices.  The implementation reuses one coefficient builder for this common
core, but currently solves and reconstructs every cell separately.  Shared
factorizations or a common dual basis are therefore plausible computational
improvements; the present timings do not claim them.

\begin{proposition}[Exact rational residual certificate]
\label{prop:rational-residual}
In this proposition only, uppercase $Q$ denotes a rational Hessian; it is
unrelated to the lowercase contraction factor $q$ in \cref{prop:horizon}.
Let $Q\in\mathbb S_{++}^n\cap\mathbb Q^{n\times n}$,
$\alpha\in\mathbb Q$, $A=I-\alpha Q$, and
$0<\alpha\lambda_j(Q)<2$ for every $j$.  Consider
$f(z)=\tfrac12 z^\top Qz$, a current state with $\norm{x}=1$, the candidate
$y=A^d x$, and gradient-norm tolerance $\eps>0$.  For $k=0,\ldots,d-1$, set
\begin{equation}
 C_k=(A^k)^\top Q^2A^k
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 p_k=\min_{W\succeq0}\{\ip{C_k}{W}:\ip{I}{W}=1\}.       \label{eq:residual-sdp}
\end{equation}
If there are rational numbers $\nu_k$ and rational symmetric matrices
$Z_k$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 C_k=\nu_k I+Z_k,\qquad Z_k\succeq0,\qquad
 \nu_k>\eps^2,                                      \label{eq:rational-dual}
\end{equation}
then $N_f(y)=N_f(x)-d$.  If, in addition, the transcript fully reveals
$(Q,x,y)$ so that its
consistency class is this singleton quadratic, then the joint continuation
gate accepts for every $m\leq d$ and $c\leq d$.  Without full revelation the
stopping-time identity is only a statement about this fixed quadratic, not
about every other function compatible with a coarser transcript.  Equations
\eqref{eq:rational-dual} are exact dual
certificates for \eqref{eq:residual-sdp}; their verification requires no
eigendecomposition or floating-point tolerance.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any primal feasible $W$, \eqref{eq:rational-dual} and weak duality give
\[
 \ip{C_k}{W}=\nu_k\ip{I}{W}+\ip{Z_k}{W}
 \geq\nu_k>\eps^2,
\]
because the trace inner product of two positive-semidefinite matrices is
nonnegative.  Taking $W=xx^\top$ yields
$\norm{QA^kx}^2=x^\top C_kx>\eps^2$ for every
$k<d$, so $N_f(x)\geq d$.  The trajectories from $x$ and $y=A^dx$ satisfy
$y_k=A^ky=A^{k+d}x=x_{k+d}$.  Since $A$ is a strict contraction, the stopping
index is finite; its first index from $y$ is exactly $N_f(x)-d$.
Consequently, under the stated full-revelation condition, the consistency set
is a singleton and has $\Delta^*=-d$; \eqref{eq:gamma} becomes
$\max\{-d+m,c-d\}\leq0$ under the stated conditions.
Finally, the dual of \eqref{eq:residual-sdp} is
$\max_\nu\{\nu:C_k-\nu I\succeq0\}$, which proves the certificate claim.
\end{proof}

For a rational symmetric matrix, positive semidefiniteness is equivalent to
nonnegativity of all principal minors.  Thus \eqref{eq:rational-dual} can be
checked using only integer arithmetic after clearing denominators.  This
criterion is exponential in $n$ and is used here for additional validation,
not proposed as the scalable online PSD test.

\section{Cost ledger and funded fallback}
\label{sec:cost}

Let $c$ include proposal, solution, verification, and rejected work in
exact-call units.  A verified suite applies to any later branch satisfying the
same envelope $(\mu,L,R,D,\delta,\eps,c,m)$; it is not tied to an identical
numerical transcript.  Offline search is amortized only across such compatible
uses, and any envelope-acquisition work is charged separately.

\begin{proposition}[Zero-credit rejection path]
\label{prop:zero-credit}
Suppose an online decision routine costs $\kappa\geq0$ and, when it rejects,
returns fallback state $z_f$.  With zero initial credit, rejection is robustly
noninferior if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{f\in\mathfrak F(\cT)}
 \{\kappa+N_f(z_f)-N_f(x)\}\leq0.                  \label{eq:fallback}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $z_f=x$ and $\kappa>0$, robust non-inferiority is impossible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On function $f$, the rejection path costs $\kappa+N_f(z_f)$ and the unchanged
baseline costs $N_f(x)$.  The former is no larger for every consistent function
if and only if their worst difference is nonpositive, which is
\eqref{eq:fallback}.  Substituting $z_f=x$ makes every difference equal to
$\kappa$.
\end{proof}

Thus an unchanged fallback cannot fund the first paid decision.  The
certificate must be prepaid, computed from sunk work, or made productive enough
that the fallback advances; this obstruction is independent of PEP tightness.

For a prepaid certificate of measured cost $T_{\rm cert}$ seconds reused for
$K$ compatible decisions, with one exact call taking $t_f$ seconds, the charge is
\begin{equation}
 c_{\rm cert}(K)=\frac{T_{\rm cert}}{K t_f}
 \quad\text{exact-call units}.                         \label{eq:cost-amortization}
\end{equation}
The H6 four-worker search takes \HsixCertificateSearchSeconds{} seconds and the
median complete standard-library replay takes
\HsixCertificateVerifySeconds{} seconds, for
$T_{\rm cert}=\HsixCertificateTotalSeconds{}$ seconds.  More generally, with
one-time envelope cost $C_{\rm env}$, measured in exact-call units, and per-branch
noncertificate cost $c_{\rm branch}<1$, complete funding requires
\begin{equation}
 c_{\rm branch}+\frac{C_{\rm env}+T_{\rm cert}/t_f}{K}\leq1,
 \qquad
 K\geq\left\lceil
 \frac{C_{\rm env}+T_{\rm cert}/t_f}{1-c_{\rm branch}}
 \right\rceil.                                       \label{eq:full-envelope-cost}
\end{equation}
Changing any envelope field requires a proved containment relation or a new
charge.  The heat-inverse experiment below replaces the earlier hypothetical
60-second conversion by measured $t_f=\HeatExactMedianSeconds{}$ seconds.  Its
prepaid proof costs \HeatCertificateUnits{} exact-call units and becomes
worthwhile only relative to the explicitly penalized unsafe comparator; it
does not beat the unpenalized baseline.

\subsection{Unified nonzero-radius dependence flagship}
\label{sec:h6-unified}

The principal acceptance experiment makes proof production and
joint-over-marginal value coexist.  It uses the infinite envelope
$\cF_{3/10,1}$ with
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  &\alpha=1,\qquad \gamma=\frac{11}{10},\qquad R=\frac95,
  \qquad \frac{27}{50}\leq\norm{y-x}\leq\frac{14}{25},\\
  &\norm{y-x+\tfrac{11}{10}\nabla f(x)}\leq\frac1{100},
  \qquad \eps=\frac7{25}.
 \end{aligned}                                      \label{eq:h6-unified}
\end{equation}
The candidate is known at deployment, but the reusable proof intentionally
uses only this coarser envelope; it applies to every candidate and function
satisfying \eqref{eq:h6-unified}.  Here $D_0=59/25$ and $q=7/10$, and
\[
 \frac{59}{25}\left(\frac7{10}\right)^5>\frac7{25}
 \geq\frac{59}{25}\left(\frac7{10}\right)^6,
\]
so the natural horizon is $H=6$.  With $c=2/5$ and $m=1$, the complete bad set
is the 28 cells $s\geq r$.

The producer submitted every bad cell without a structural prescreen.  It
recovered \HsixJointCertificates{} rational duals, whose largest exact upper
bound is \HsixJointUpper{}$<0$.  The standard-library consumer reconstructs up to
\HsixJointInequalities{} inequality rows and order-\HsixJointGram{} slack
matrices from the parameter tuple and checks \HsixJointPivots{} positive
rational $LDL^\top$ pivots.  Weak duality excludes all $s\geq r$, while the
witnesses below attain $s-r=-1$; hence $\Delta^*=-1$ and the joint gate accepts
at boundary value zero.

The exact separately conditioned marginals make a different decision.  The envelope
implies
\[
 \frac{53}{110}\leq\norm{\nabla f(x)}\leq\frac{57}{110},
\]
because the residual ball and proposal band give
\[
 \frac{27}{50}-\frac1{100}
 \leq\frac{11}{10}\norm{\nabla f(x)}
 \leq\frac{14}{25}+\frac1{100}.
\]
Hence $L_x\geq1$.  For the exact proposal center
$T_{11/10}x=x-(11/10)\nabla f(x)$, the averaged-Hessian argument in
\cref{prop:horizon} gives
\[
 \norm{\nabla f(T_{11/10}x)}
 \leq\frac{67}{100}\norm{\nabla f(x)}.
\]
The residual triangle inequality and $L=1$ therefore give
\begin{align*}
 \norm{\nabla f(y)}
 \leq\frac{67}{100}\frac{57}{110}+\frac1{100}
 &=\frac{3929}{11000},\\
 \norm{\nabla f(y_1)}\leq\frac7{10}\frac{3929}{11000}
 &=\frac{27503}{110000}<\frac7{25}.
\end{align*}
Therefore $U_y\leq1$.  Both bounds are sharp within the same declared
envelope: the one-dimensional quadratics
\[
 f_1(t)=\frac12t^2+\frac12t,\quad y=-\frac{11}{20},
 \qquad

\]

\subsection{Feasible regime and calibration boundary}
\label{sec:feasible-regime}

The horizon, rather than physical dimension, is the immediate computational
bottleneck.  For the common step $\alpha=1/L$, writing
$\kappa=L/\mu$ gives $q=1-1/\kappa$ and, for a distance envelope $D$,
\begin{equation}
 H=\left\lceil
 \frac{\log(\eps/(LD))}{\log(1-1/\kappa)}
 \right\rceil
 =\Theta\!\left(\kappa\log\frac{LD}{\eps}\right)
 \quad(\kappa\to\infty).                              \label{eq:horizon-scaling}
\end{equation}
There are $(H+1)^2$ nominal cells and, without additional structure,
$\Theta(H^2)$ can be cost violating.  A ragged cell $(r,s)$ has Gram order
$r+s+4\leq2H+4$ and $\Theta((r+s)^2)$ ordered interpolation inequalities.
Even the optimistic accounting of one dense $O(H^3)$ factorization per cell
therefore gives $O(H^5)$ work for exhaustive enumeration; a generic
interior-point method can be more expensive because its affine-constraint
system is itself of order $H^2$.  This is a scaling diagnosis, not a solver
complexity theorem.

The experiments expose this boundary: the exact suite stops at $H=10$.  The
implemented four-worker ragged builder reaches $H=15$, enumerating
\HfifteenCells{} bad cells in \HfifteenWallSeconds{} seconds with maximum Gram
order \HfifteenMaxGram{} and \HfifteenMaxConstraints{} constraints, but peaks
at \HfifteenPeakRss{} MiB.  Clarabel reports \HfifteenOptimal{} optimal and
\HfifteenInaccurate{} optimal-inaccurate cells; because neither status is an
exact exclusion, the run is \emph{uncertified}.  At $H=20$ the generic run
reaches \GenericPepBadCells{} bad cells and Gram order \GenericPepMaxGram{},
and likewise remains uncertified.  The short-transcript SPX bound
has candidate horizon \PositiveSpxShortHybridUpper{} and was not enumerated.
Likewise, the 2,000 finite proxies are reported only as a dependence-loss audit,
not as calibration of the infinite-class backend; their detailed horizon counts
remain in the artifact.

One proposed scaling mechanism is implemented rather than left as a roadmap.
A single Disciplined Parametrized Programming model pads all 136 H15 bad cells,
changes only terminal/survival selector parameters, reuses canonicalization,
and warm-starts consecutive solves.  It completes in
\BatchedHfifteenWallSeconds{} seconds at \BatchedHfifteenPeakRss{} MiB.  Against
the frozen four-worker ragged run, peak memory falls to
\BatchedHfifteenRssRatio{} (a \BatchedHfifteenMemoryReduction{} reduction),
but wall time rises by \BatchedHfifteenWallRatio{} times.  Three sampled cells
agree with separately rebuilt ragged models within
\BatchedHfifteenMaxDifference{}.  This is a concrete cross-cell model-reuse
tradeoff, not an exact H15 acceptance: all solver labels remain outside the
trust boundary.

This also separates information from implementation: the exact-cell optimality
theorem characterizes the best deterministic decision given the exact
attainable set;
it is not a polynomial-time claim.  When the necessary cell proofs cannot be
constructed, the implemented gate returns \emph{uncertified}.  Information-
theoretic optimality and computational nonavailability can therefore coexist,
as the $H=20$ audit demonstrates.

Accordingly, C2OGate is presently a moderate-horizon exact-certification
infrastructure, not a scalable generic SDP solver.  The validated envelope is
$H\leq10$, Gram order at most 24, and at most 534 inequalities per cell.  A
remaining scaling program has three separately testable components: exploit the
interpolation graph by chordal Gram decompositions; batch coefficient assembly
and reuse a dual basis across cells that differ only in stopping rows; and
replace bounded-denominator rounding by validated facial reduction or interval
residual correction on degenerate faces.  The parameterized H15 experiment
implements model and canonicalization reuse, but not chordal decomposition,
dual-basis reuse, or facial reduction; it also remains outside the acceptance
path.  If the $H=10$ producer had
failed, the finite SDP reduction,
three-valued kernel, and verifier would remain valid, but the principal
moderate-horizon acceptance evidence would fall back to the $H=3$ suites.  The
computational contribution therefore depends empirically---and transparently---
on successful recovery at the reported scale.

measured warm ratio is \UciWdbcMeasuredWarmRatio{}; accepting every free
prefilter attempt has lower ratio \UciWdbcGreedyMeasuredRatio{} and no observed

These facts refute any claim that certification improves mean selection on the
two convenient workloads.  Their value is integration coverage: every class,
proposal, residual, and distance predicate is recomputed exactly from stored
binary64 input before the certificate is reused.

The positive cost experiment is a periodic two-dimensional heat inverse
problem.  For two Fourier coefficients $z$, the expensive oracle evaluates a
$640\times640$ forward heat solve for 120 explicit steps and the corresponding
adjoint, plus a $0.3I$ ridge.  The exact-real Hessian eigenvalues are
$0.9980596$ and $0.4417816$, so the objective lies in
$\cF_{3/10,1}$.  A fixed $80\times80$, three-step surrogate has eigenvalues
$0.9968984$ and $0.3728483$: it resolves the low mode but over-diffuses the
high mode.  The full and cheap median oracle times are
\HeatExactMedianSeconds{} and \HeatCheapMedianSeconds{} seconds, respectively;
the maximum grid-gradient discrepancy from the diagonal Fourier formula is
$4.0\times10^{-15}$.

\section{Implementation limits and extensions}

The guarantee is exact only relative to the declared class, transcript, and
cost model.  A false cheap-oracle contract places the realized function outside
$\mathfrak F(\cT)$; a missing distance bound can make the grid unbounded; and a
physical dimension below the Gram rank requires the rank qualification of
\cref{thm:sdp-exact}.  The interface rejects unsupported nonlinear predicates
rather than silently relaxing them.  The two short-transcript runners perform
membership and class checks exactly relative to their stored binary64 inputs;
uncertainty upstream of those stored values remains outside the guarantee.

Constant acquisition remains the main application bottleneck.
\Cref{prop:transcript-pipeline} makes $R,D,\delta$ auditable when a gradient and
$\mu$ are already certified, but it does not make global curvature cheap.
Deployment must inflate a measured residual by its error bound and apply
\cref{prop:radius-error}; the present H6 family certifies through $7/500$ but is
already uncertified at $2/125$.  UCI and rolling controls have cheaper
prefilter-only mean cost and zero observed overruns.  The heat experiment adds
one positive, fully measured ledger, but only under a declared 12-call penalty
per failed saving at the observed 64 decisions.  Unpenalized break-even needs
\HeatUnpenalizedAmortizationEpisodes{} compatible decisions; neither threshold
establishes production frequency.  The intended regime is therefore a repeated, audited PDE or
calibration pipeline with reusable envelopes and an explicit aversion to
pointwise cost overruns, not a one-off problem with millisecond gradients.

Computationally, generic $H=15$ and $H=20$ recovery remains
\emph{uncertified}.  Parameterized cross-cell reuse is implemented and reduces
H15 peak memory by \BatchedHfifteenMemoryReduction{}, but is
\BatchedHfifteenWallRatio{} times slower; chordal sparsity, dual-basis reuse,
and facial reduction remain unimplemented.  The reusable contribution is
consequently the semantic schema, external-consumer pattern, three-valued
aggregator, and ledger, not a claim that the producer crosses the exact
recovery wall.

Extensions to proximal, primal--dual, or active-manifold methods require the
corresponding exact interpolation or identification certificate
\citep{Bousselmi2024}.  Adaptive steps additionally make a cell depend on the
realized acceptance history.  No generic index-dominance pruning rule is
claimed: only proved transcript identities remove cells.  These are substantive
new models, not mechanical uses of the present fixed-step verifier.

\section{Conclusion}

C2OGate converts one continuation choice into a three-valued,
certificate-carrying computation.  It preserves joint stopping dependence,
reconstructs the SDP outside the numerical producer, and accepts only after
every cost-violating cell has an exact exclusion.  The $H=6$ infinite-class
result accepts while sharp marginals reject; a second suite enlarges the
proposal radius by 40\%, and a nonsharing SymPy implementation plus randomized
exact differential tests strengthen the trust boundary.  The $H=10$ suite is
the larger proof-production load.  A generic PEPit-to-rational baseline
certifies 5/28 H6 cells, quantifying the native representation's recovery
advantage.  Parameterized H15 reuse reduces memory but not time.  The measured
heat inverse gives explicit adoption thresholds of
\HeatAmortizationEpisodes{} risk-adjusted or
\HeatUnpenalizedAmortizationEpisodes{} unpenalized compatible decisions.

This is moderate-horizon infrastructure, not a universal accelerator.  Dual
discovery is heuristic, the consumer is still trusted code, $H=15/20$ recovery
is uncertified, and no workload establishes production acceptance frequency.
Within those boundaries, the method provides an exact, replayable way to claim
pointwise cost dominance when the function class, transcript, funding source,
and cost units can themselves be certified.
